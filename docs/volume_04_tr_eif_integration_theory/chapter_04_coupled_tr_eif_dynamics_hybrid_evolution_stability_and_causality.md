# Coupled TR-EIF Dynamics, Hybrid Evolution, Stability, and Causality

## 1. Purpose

This document formalizes the coupled dynamical layer of the Ternary Resonant Equivariant Interatomic Framework after completion of the forward and reverse integration mappings.

The previously established bidirectional chain is:

`EIF state`

`→ EIF output`

`→ EIF-to-TR mapping`

`→ TR input`

`→ TR evolution`

`→ resonance processing`

`→ ternary target`

`→ neutral-mediated -1/0/1 execution`

`→ TR output`

`→ TR-to-EIF mapping`

`→ EIF update`

`→ updated EIF state`

This chapter formalizes the complete coupled evolution generated by that chain.

It establishes:

- complete coupled state;
- continuous, discrete, and hybrid state components;
- coupled evolution operators;
- event semantics;
- update ordering;
- synchronous and asynchronous coupling;
- multirate coupling;
- cross-layer delay;
- retained memory;
- causality;
- algebraic loops;
- fixed points;
- periodic solutions;
- local stability;
- perturbation semantics;
- linearization boundaries;
- switched and hybrid stability boundaries;
- Lyapunov-function interfaces;
- energy and conservativity boundaries;
- dissipative coupling;
- multiscale coupled dynamics;
- deterministic replay;
- trajectory semantics;
- coupled observables;
- stability-validation requirements;
- strict separation between dynamical stability, resonance classification, ternary execution, structural change, and physical phase transition.

No universal stability criterion, timestep, feedback gain, delay, damping constant, or physical coupling parameter is introduced.

## 4. Coupled State Space

Let:

`S_C`

denote the complete coupled TR-EIF state space.

A generic coupled state is:

`s_C ∈ S_C`

A minimally explicit construction is:

`S_C = S_EIF × S_TR × H_ET × H_TE × S_X`

where:

- `S_EIF` is the EIF state space;
- `S_TR` is the TR state space;
- `H_ET` is forward cross-layer history;
- `H_TE` is reverse cross-layer history;
- `S_X` contains additional retained integration state.

Unused factors may be omitted by a specialization.

## 5. Additional Integration State

`S_X`

may contain:

- routing state;
- synchronization state;
- cross-layer queue state;
- rate-limit state;
- coupling-mode state;
- event state;
- cross-layer topology;
- multirate scheduler state.

Any result-affecting retained variable belongs to the coupled state contract.

## 6. Product Space Does Not Define Dynamics

The product:

`S_EIF × S_TR`

contains both layer states.

It does not define how either state evolves.

Coupling requires explicit evolution relations.

Therefore:

`coupled state space ≠ coupled dynamics`

## 7. Continuous Coupled State

Let:

`s_c(t)`

denote the continuous component of coupled state.

Its state space is:

`S_C,c`

A continuous evolution law may be:

`ds_c / dt = F_C(s_c, s_d, h_C, u_C, λ_C)`

where:

- `s_d` is discrete coupled state;
- `h_C` is retained history;
- `u_C` is external input;
- `λ_C` is parameter state.

## 8. Discrete Coupled State

Let:

`s_d[k] ∈ S_C,d`

denote discrete state at event or execution index `k`.

It may contain:

- executed ternary state;
- pending ternary destination;
- discrete routing state;
- topology class;
- coupling mode;
- discrete scheduler state.

## 9. Hybrid Coupled State

A general TR-EIF realization may therefore use:

`s_C = (s_c, s_d, h_C)`

with continuous evolution between events and discrete updates at event times.

## 10. Hybrid Character

TR-EIF can be hybrid because:

- EIF may contain continuous interatomic dynamics;
- TR may contain continuous phase or resonance variables;
- TR ternary execution is discrete;
- topology may change discretely;
- cross-layer feedback may trigger discrete update events.

No individual specialization is required to contain every component.

## 11. Continuous Flow Domain

Let:

`C_C ⊆ S_C`

be the set of coupled states in which continuous evolution is permitted.

A flow relation is defined only on:

`C_C`

## 12. Event Guard Set

Let:

`D_C ⊆ S_C`

be the event or jump domain.

A state entering `D_C` may trigger a declared discrete update.

## 13. Jump Mapping

Define:

`J_C: D_C → S_C`

or, more generally:

`J_C: D_C × X_J × Λ_J → S_C`

The jump mapping may update:

- TR ternary state;
- pending route;
- cross-layer routing;
- EIF feedback state;
- topology state;
- other declared discrete components.

## 14. Guard Is Not Event Result

A guard determines event eligibility.

It does not determine the resulting state unless the jump map is also defined.

Therefore:

`guard ≠ state update`

## 15. TR Target Is Not Hybrid Guard Automatically

A ternary target may participate in a jump condition.

It is not itself the entire hybrid guard.

## 16. Neutral-Mediation Guard

For opposite-polarity execution, the hybrid event rules must preserve:

`-1 → 0`

and later:

`0 → 1`

or:

`1 → 0`

and later:

`0 → -1`

No hybrid formulation may collapse those two events into one direct opposite transition.

## 17. Coupled Update Operator

For a discrete-time specialization, define:

`Φ_C: S_C × X_C,in × Λ_C → S_C`

with:

`s_C[n + 1] = Φ_C(s_C[n], x_C[n], λ_C)`

The complete operator must encode all result-affecting cross-layer operations.

## 18. Factorized Coupled Update

A coupled update may be represented as a composition:

`Φ_C = Φ_E ∘ Φ_TE ∘ Φ_T ∘ Φ_ET`

where:

- `Φ_ET` evaluates the forward cross-layer path;
- `Φ_T` updates TR state;
- `Φ_TE` evaluates reverse feedback;
- `Φ_E` updates EIF state.

The composition order is part of the model.

## 19. Alternative Ordering

A different composition order defines a different coupled update in general.

Therefore:

`Φ_E ∘ Φ_TE ∘ Φ_T ∘ Φ_ET`

is not assumed equal to:

`Φ_T ∘ Φ_ET ∘ Φ_E ∘ Φ_TE`

## 20. Update Ordering Is State Semantics

If order affects the result, it belongs to the formal model rather than only implementation documentation.

## 21. Synchronous Coupling

A synchronous discrete specialization evaluates designated cross-layer operations on a common execution index.

The exact sequence within each index must still be declared.

## 22. Asynchronous Coupling

An asynchronous specialization permits EIF and TR updates at different event times.

Each event must identify:

- source time;
- destination time;
- retained input;
- update rule.

## 23. Multirate Coupling

Let EIF updates occur at times:

`{t_E,k}`

and TR updates at:

`{t_T,m}`

The two sets need not coincide.

A multirate contract must define cross-layer value availability between these times.

## 24. Sample-and-Hold

One multirate strategy is to retain the most recent cross-layer value until the next source update.

This is a sample-and-hold rule.

It creates a specific temporal approximation.

## 25. Interpolation

A specialization may instead interpolate source values between samples.

The interpolation rule is part of the numerical realization or mathematical model, depending on its role.

## 26. Extrapolation

Using future-estimated source values is extrapolation.

It must not be documented as direct observation.

## 27. Synchronization Point

A synchronization point is an execution coordinate at which selected layer states are mutually available under the coupling contract.

It is not TR synchronization in the phase-dynamics sense.

Therefore:

`execution synchronization point ≠ oscillator synchronization`

## 28. Cross-Layer Delay

Let:

`τ_ET ≥ 0`

denote a forward delay where continuous delay is explicitly defined.

A delayed forward dependence may use:

`y_E(t - τ_ET)`

Likewise:

`τ_TE ≥ 0`

may define reverse delay.

## 29. Forward and Reverse Delays Are Independent

In general:

`τ_ET ≠ τ_TE`

No symmetry between them is assumed.

## 30. Delay Is Not Phase Lag

The distinction remains mandatory:

`delay ≠ phase lag`

A Sakaguchi-type phase lag is not a transmission delay unless separately modeled as one.

## 31. Delay Requires History

For maximum delay:

`τ_max > 0`

the state specification must include sufficient history over an interval such as:

`[t_0 - τ_max, t_0]`

or an equivalent retained representation.

## 32. Discrete Delay

For integer delay:

`d_ET ≥ 0`

a discrete forward path may depend on:

`y_E[n - d_ET]`

The corresponding buffer is part of state.

## 33. Memory

Memory exists whenever future coupled evolution depends on retained past information not reconstructible from instantaneous visible state alone.

Such retained information must be included in `S_C`.

## 34. Hidden Memory Is Forbidden

A deterministic implementation with an undocumented stateful filter is not fully represented by a memoryless mathematical specification.

## 35. Hysteresis

A cross-layer or internal classification with path-dependent branch state introduces hysteresis.

The branch state belongs to the coupled state.

## 36. Pending Ternary Route as Memory

A pending opposite destination is a concrete retained TR memory object.

It affects future execution and therefore belongs to `S_TR`.

## 37. Pending Route Is Not Delay Buffer

Pending destination and delayed source history are different state objects.

## 38. Causal Dependency

A mapping is causal with respect to a declared time coordinate when its output at time `t` depends only on state and inputs available at or before `t`.

## 39. Causality Requires Time Semantics

A graph edge between EIF and TR objects does not itself establish temporal causality.

Time or event ordering must be defined.

## 40. Forward Causal Relation

A causal forward relation may be represented as:

`EIF history up to t`

`→ TR input at t`

## 41. Reverse Causal Relation

A causal reverse relation may be:

`TR history up to t`

`+ EIF context at or before t`

`→ EIF update at t`

## 42. Closed-Loop Causal Cycle

A feedback cycle across multiple times is not a logical contradiction:

`EIF[t]`

`→ TR[t + Δ1]`

`→ EIF[t + Δ1 + Δ2]`

The time ordering resolves the dependence.

## 43. Algebraic Loop

An algebraic loop occurs when current EIF and TR values depend instantaneously on one another without an explicit temporal ordering:

`x = F(y)`

`y = G(x)`

Such a relation requires a simultaneous solution rule.

## 44. Algebraic Loop Is Not Feedback Delay

An algebraic loop is instantaneous in the model.

A delayed feedback path is temporally ordered.

They must not be conflated.

## 45. Algebraic Solution

An algebraic loop may require solving:

`R(z) = 0`

for a coupled unknown:

`z`

The existence and uniqueness of a solution require separate analysis.

## 46. Causal Graph

A coupled implementation may define a directed dependency graph:

`G_Causal`

whose nodes are state or operation classes and whose edges indicate result-affecting dependence.

The graph must not be interpreted as a physical interaction network automatically.

## 47. Computational Causality and Physical Causality

An implementation dependency shows computational causality inside the model.

It does not by itself establish a fundamental physical causal mechanism.

## 48. Coupled Trajectory

A coupled trajectory is an ordered state evolution:

`τ_C = {s_C(t)}`

for continuous time or:

`τ_C = (s_C[0], s_C[1], ..., s_C[N])`

for discrete execution.

## 49. Coupled Trajectory Contains Both Layers

A complete coupled trajectory preserves both:

- EIF state evolution;
- TR state evolution.

Recording only one layer is a partial trajectory projection.

## 50. Cross-Layer Event Trace

An event trace may record:

- EIF observation;
- forward-map evaluation;
- TR input registration;
- resonance-classification event;
- ternary target event;
- ternary execution leg;
- reverse-map evaluation;
- EIF update request;
- EIF update execution.

## 51. Event Trace Is Not State Trajectory

Event trace and state trajectory may be related but are different artifacts.

## 52. Causal Traceability

A closed-loop claim should permit the chain:

`source state`

`→ forward transformation`

`→ TR event`

`→ reverse transformation`

`→ EIF state change`

to be traced without semantic gaps.

## 53. Fixed Point

For a discrete coupled update:

`Φ_C`

a fixed point is:

`s_C*`

satisfying:

`Φ_C(s_C*) = s_C*`

## 54. Fixed Point Is Full Coupled State

A fixed point applies to the complete state included in `Φ_C`.

An unchanged ternary state alone does not establish a coupled fixed point.

## 55. Continuous Equilibrium

For continuous dynamics:

`ds_C / dt = F_C(s_C)`

an equilibrium satisfies:

`F_C(s_C*) = 0`

for all continuous state components, together with consistency of any discrete state.

## 56. Equilibrium Is Not Necessarily Ternary Neutral

A coupled equilibrium may have executed ternary state:

`-1`

`0`

or:

`1`

depending on the model.

Therefore:

`equilibrium ≠ ternary 0`

## 57. Ternary Neutral Is Not Mechanical Equilibrium

Likewise:

`σ = 0`

does not imply zero EIF force or zero EIF velocity.

## 58. Fixed Point Is Not Resonance Automatically

A fixed point of the coupled state-update map does not automatically lie:

`INSIDE`

a resonance window.

## 59. Resonance Is Not Fixed-Point Stability

Resonance classification and local dynamical stability answer different questions.

## 60. Periodic Orbit

A discrete state:

`s_C`

belongs to a period-`p` orbit when:

`Φ_C^p(s_C) = s_C`

and `p` is the minimal positive period.

## 61. Periodic Orbit Is Not Resonance Automatically

A periodic coupled trajectory does not establish TR resonance by itself.

## 62. Periodic Ternary Pattern

A repeating ternary execution sequence is a discrete periodic pattern.

It is not automatically a physical oscillation or resonance.

## 63. Perturbation

Let:

`s_C*`

be a reference state.

A perturbation:

`δs`

must belong to an admissible local coordinate or tangent representation of the coupled state.

Discrete-state perturbations require separate treatment.

## 64. Continuous Perturbation

For continuous state component:

`x`

a perturbed state may be:

`x + δx`

when the local state space permits such addition.

## 65. Discrete Perturbation

Changing ternary state from:

`0`

to:

`1`

is not an infinitesimal perturbation.

It is a discrete state change.

## 66. Hybrid Perturbation Structure

Hybrid stability may therefore require separate analysis of:

- continuous perturbations within one discrete mode;
- discrete mode transitions;
- event-boundary perturbations.

## 67. Local Stability

A reference solution is locally stable only under a declared metric, neighborhood, and dynamical model.

The term `stable` is incomplete without these definitions.

## 68. Stability Space

Let:

`d_C`

be a metric or comparison structure on the relevant coupled state space.

The chosen metric must respect the mixed continuous/discrete semantics.

## 69. Mixed-State Distance Boundary

A Euclidean norm over raw serialized state is not automatically a valid metric for:

- ternary state;
- topology;
- categorical state;
- periodic phase;
- dimensional physical variables.

## 70. Phase Distance

Circular phase requires circular distance semantics.

A raw difference across the `2π` wrap boundary can misrepresent phase proximity.

## 71. Ternary Distance

A ternary state comparison is discrete.

It must not be mixed dimensionally with physical Euclidean distance without a declared composite metric.

## 72. Composite Stability Metric

A coupled metric may combine several state components only after each component metric and weighting has been defined.

Any weights require provenance.

## 73. Local Asymptotic Stability

A continuous equilibrium may be called locally asymptotically stable only when nearby admissible trajectories remain close and converge to the equilibrium under the declared model.

This property is model-specific.

## 74. Discrete Local Stability

For differentiable discrete map near a fixed point within one smooth mode, local behavior may be studied through the Jacobian:

`J_C = DΦ_C(s_C*)`

on the relevant continuous coordinates.

## 75. Linearization Boundary

Linearization applies only in a neighborhood where:

- the map is differentiable;
- the same discrete mode remains active;
- event boundaries are not crossed unexpectedly.

## 76. Jacobian Does Not Describe Discrete Jumps Fully

A continuous-state Jacobian does not encode all behavior of ternary mode transitions.

Hybrid event maps require separate treatment.

## 77. Spectral Local Criterion Boundary

For a smooth discrete map, eigenvalues of the local Jacobian may characterize local linear behavior.

Such a criterion is not automatically valid across discontinuous switching boundaries.

## 78. Continuous Linearization

For continuous flow:

`ds / dt = F_C(s)`

the local Jacobian:

`A_C = DF_C(s_C*)`

describes the first-order local approximation in a smooth mode.

## 79. Local Linear Approximation Is Not Global Model

Therefore:

`linearized stability ≠ global stability`

## 80. Stability Margin Boundary

A numerical distance of eigenvalues from a local stability boundary is a model-dependent local quantity.

It is not a universal TR-EIF stability constant.

## 81. Lyapunov Function

A candidate Lyapunov function is a scalar mapping:

`V: D_V → ℝ`

defined on a neighborhood or state domain.

Its use requires declared positivity and evolution properties appropriate to the selected stability claim.

## 82. Lyapunov Function Is Not Energy Automatically

A Lyapunov function may coincide with physical energy in some models.

It need not.

Therefore:

`Lyapunov function ≠ physical energy automatically`

## 83. Physical Energy as Lyapunov Candidate

A dissipative physical system may admit an energy-like Lyapunov function under specific assumptions.

Those assumptions must be established.

## 84. Ternary State Is Not Lyapunov Value

The values:

`-1/0/1`

do not encode Lyapunov stability.

## 85. Resonance Classification Is Not Lyapunov Stability

`INSIDE`

a resonance window does not automatically mean dynamically stable.

`OUTSIDE`

does not automatically mean unstable.

## 86. Coherence Is Not Stability

High coherence does not establish local or global dynamical stability.

## 87. Phase Order Is Not Stability

High:

`R(t)`

does not establish stability.

The invariant:

`R(t) ≠ C(t)`

also remains in force.

## 88. Boundedness

A trajectory is bounded with respect to a declared norm or metric when it remains inside a bounded region.

Boundedness is weaker than convergence in general.

## 89. Boundedness Is Not Stability Automatically

A bounded trajectory may fail other stability definitions.

## 90. Convergence

Convergence must define:

- target set;
- metric;
- time or iteration limit;
- applicable domain.

## 91. Convergence to Set

A hybrid system may converge to a set rather than one point.

The target set must be explicitly defined.

## 92. Limit Cycle Boundary

A stable periodic orbit is different from a stable equilibrium.

Neither is automatically resonance.

## 93. Switched Dynamics

Different ternary states may select different continuous EIF or TR update laws.

A specialization may therefore define mode-indexed dynamics:

`F_(-1)`

`F_0`

`F_1`

This is an author-defined switched-system structure when used.

## 94. Mode-Specific Dynamics Are Not Implied

The existence of ternary states does not automatically mean the physical dynamics differ among them.

A mapping must define any mode-specific effect.

## 95. Active Neutral Mode

If a specialization associates a distinct dynamic mode with:

`0`

that mode must preserve active-neutral semantics.

It must not be documented as an absent mode.

## 96. Mode Switching

A change in ternary executed state may switch the active feedback law.

This is a control or hybrid-system event.

It is not automatically a structural transition.

## 97. Switching Stability Boundary

Stability of each isolated mode does not guarantee stability under arbitrary switching.

The switching sequence and transition rules matter.

## 98. Neutral Mediation Constrains Switching

TR transition invariants constrain the admissible mode-switching graph:

`-1 ↔ 1`

has no direct execution edge.

The path must pass through:

`0`

## 99. Ternary Transition Graph

The admissible executed-state graph contains edges:

`-1 → 0`

`0 → -1`

`0 → 1`

`1 → 0`

and may contain self-retention according to the selected model.

It excludes:

`-1 → 1`

and:

`1 → -1`

## 100. Switching Graph Is Not Physical Topology

The ternary transition graph is an execution-state graph.

It is not an interatomic interaction topology.

## 101. Dwell Time

A switched specialization may impose or observe a dwell interval in one mode.

Any required minimum dwell time must be explicitly defined.

## 102. Neutral Retention Is Not Fixed Dwell Time Automatically

TR neutral may persist arbitrarily under the base invariant.

A fixed neutral dwell requirement is an additional model rule.

## 103. Feedback Saturation and Stability

Saturation can change the closed-loop vector field or update map.

Local analysis performed without saturation may fail outside the unsaturated region.

## 104. Rate Limiting and Stability

Rate limits introduce additional state or nonlinearity when future output depends on previous applied update.

Such state must be included in stability analysis.

## 105. Queue Dynamics

Queued cross-layer updates introduce another dynamical subsystem.

Queue occupancy, priority, and pending requests may affect future coupled state.

## 106. Queue Stability Is Not Physical Stability

A bounded communication queue does not imply a physically stable interatomic system.

## 107. Capacity Constraint

Finite cross-layer processing capacity can introduce delay and backlog.

Capacity must be included when it is result-affecting.

## 108. Conservative EIF Baseline

Suppose isolated EIF dynamics is derived from:

`E_EIF(q)`

with:

`F_EIF = -grad_q E_EIF`

This defines a conservative baseline under the applicable assumptions.

## 109. Coupled Energy Modification

A TR-dependent energy coupling may define:

`E_C(q, s_T) = E_EIF(q) + E_X(q, s_T)`

where `E_X` has compatible energy units.

## 110. State-Dependent Energy Coupling

If `s_T` evolves independently of `q`, the total coupled dynamics may exchange energy between subsystems even when force on `q` is derived from `E_C`.

Conservation must be analyzed on the complete coupled state.

## 111. Direct Force Feedback

A direct force correction:

`F_C = F_EIF + F_X`

does not imply the existence of a scalar:

`E_C`

with:

`F_C = -grad_q E_C`

## 112. Conservativity Boundary

Therefore:

`closed-loop force feedback ≠ conservative coupling automatically`

## 113. Dissipative Feedback

A reverse map may intentionally introduce dissipation.

The dissipative term must be physically and dimensionally typed.

## 114. Dissipation Is Not Neutrality

Dissipation remains distinct from TR active neutral `0`.

## 115. External Work Boundary

If TR-controlled feedback acts as an external physical input to EIF, energy changes in EIF may represent work exchange under a declared physical relation.

EIF energy conservation is then not the appropriate isolated-system expectation.

## 116. Complete Energy Accounting

A conservation claim for the coupled system must include every modeled energy reservoir and work channel relevant to the claim.

## 117. Closed-Loop Physical Stability

A physical stability claim requires a physical model, not only algorithmic boundedness.

It must identify:

- physical state;
- perturbation;
- stability criterion;
- boundary conditions;
- validation evidence.

## 118. Numerical Stability

A numerical solver may be stable or unstable independently of the physical closed-loop system.

Therefore:

`numerical stability ≠ coupled physical stability`

## 119. Solver Stability Boundary

An unstable numerical trajectory can result from timestep or solver choice even if the underlying continuous model is stable.

## 120. Physical Instability Boundary

Conversely, a numerically well-behaved solver can accurately reproduce a physically unstable model.

## 121. Resonance-Regime Change

A coupled trajectory may cross:

`∂W_R`

and change resonance classification.

This is a resonance-regime event.

## 122. Resonance-Regime Change Is Not Bifurcation

The existing distinction remains:

`resonance-window crossing ≠ bifurcation`

## 123. Bifurcation

A bifurcation requires a qualitative change in a parameterized dynamical system under mathematically appropriate evidence.

The relevant parameter and solution structure must be defined.

## 124. Feedback Parameter

A feedback gain or coupling parameter may serve as a bifurcation parameter in a specific model.

Its mere variation does not establish a bifurcation.

## 125. Named Bifurcation Boundary

A named class such as saddle-node, Hopf, pitchfork, or period-doubling requires class-specific evidence.

Visual trajectory change is insufficient.

## 126. Ternary Transition Is Not Bifurcation

An executed:

`-1 → 0`

or:

`0 → 1`

event is a discrete state transition.

It is not a bifurcation by itself.

## 127. Structural Transition

A structural transition belongs to the EIF structural-state semantics under an independently defined structural criterion.

## 128. Structural Transition Is Not Ternary Transition

A ternary event may contribute to feedback causing structural change.

The two remain separate events.

## 129. Physical Phase Transition

A physical phase transition requires independently defined physical or thermodynamic evidence.

Neither resonance classification nor ternary switching supplies that evidence automatically.

## 130. Coupled Multiscale State

Let:

`L_E`

be EIF scales and:

`L_T`

be TR scales.

The coupled multiscale state may include:

`S_C,MS = S_E,MS × S_T,MS × H_MS`

with explicit cross-scale relations.

## 131. Cross-Layer Scale Map

Forward and reverse scale relations remain:

`C_scale,ET ⊆ L_E × L_T`

and:

`C_scale,TE ⊆ L_T × L_E`

They need not be inverses.

## 132. Multiscale Dynamics

Different scales may evolve under different update rates or state equations.

The coupling contract must preserve scale identity.

## 133. Fine-to-Coarse Delay

Information aggregated from a fine EIF scale into coarse TR scale may introduce processing or model delay.

That delay must be explicit if result-affecting.

## 134. Coarse-to-Fine Feedback

A global or coarse TR state may affect several fine EIF components through an expansion map.

The expansion does not reconstruct information absent from the coarse state.

## 135. Scale Coupling and Stability

Cross-scale feedback can alter stability even when each scale is stable in isolation.

## 136. Scale Stability Is Not Global Stability

Stability of all independently analyzed scales does not prove stability of their coupled interaction.

## 137. Integrated Transformation Action

Let:

`G_sym`

act on the complete coupled state through:
